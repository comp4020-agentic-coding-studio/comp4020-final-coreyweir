The breakthrough that moved the work forward was allowing myself to detach a little. Given the number of features and modifications I wanted
to get in, I decided that I wouldn't spend my time testing and retesting after every single change and trying to get the UX just right.
My priority had to be getting the key features in and working. I can refine the UI later and make the experience fit my expectations, but
before that I need to get the features in.

This work made me realise that sometimes I need to be the realistic developer, not the perfectionist. There'll be time for refinement later,
but if I spend too much time chasing perfection at this stage of development, I'll never get there. I think that's a lesson that is just as
relevant to professional development work: when there are time constraints, don't ship slop, but don't let the perfect be the enemy of the
good.
